The Engine & the Index

Ten dimensions of language measured as code, and the governed record that decided what the numbers meant.

What happenedTen dimensions running as deterministic code, a measured voice baseline for every flagship surface, and a record schema that went from one product area to company-wide in about six months. Central content design owns it now.

What I did

How I built the Engine

  • Measurement from published research. I implemented the ten dimensions as deterministic code from published computational-linguistics sources, so every number has a method that can be checked.
  • Baselines from shipped strings. I built each surface's band from its own production copy, five flagships first, then the rest of Meta's products through the same pipeline.
  • A spec that sorts authority. I wrote the two-layer specification that keeps every checkable rule in code and hands the model only what belongs to judgment.
  • One MCP server, kept warm. I put Verso's computation on Meta's serverless infrastructure as an MCP server: spaCy, the ten dimension engines, and the index beside them for fast comparison. That is what let Verso check its own drafts in a loop before recommending anything.

What the Engine measured, the Index governed: which numbers became targets was never the Engine's call.

Where it lives

The engine exists where content design, computational linguistics, and product overlap

Content design had the standards, but wrote them for a person to feel. Computational linguistics could measure register, rhythm, and tone, but had no reason to point any of it at a button. Product had the strings, and no way to tell whether they sounded like the brand. The engine only exists in the piece all three share: voice, stated as a target a machine can check and a team can argue with.

Instruments

The Engine read drafts against baselines built from shipped strings

"Sound confident" is an adjective. These were its instruments: deterministic checks for the rules, and computed linguistic evidence for the voice.

Those baselines didn't exist before Verso. There was no dataset of Meta's voice, and making one was the real work: I swept the codebases of the five flagships first (Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook, Meta Quest, and Horizon), building a taxonomy of situations for each surface, because notifications aren't errors and errors aren't onboarding. The same pipeline then filled out the rest of Meta's products.

The methods, the spec, and the stack · for the people who want the receipts

The sources

Every dimension sits on research someone else already published

The engine measures; it never generates. That's why its output can be argued with in public: a number has a method behind it, and a method can be shown to be wrong.

Biber · 1988 · Register variation

Sixty-seven linguistic features that cluster into dimensions of register, replicated across more than twenty languages. The reason “formal” is a measurement and not a mood.

Searle · 1976 · Speech acts

Commands, promises, apologies, assertions. A button label and an error message are different acts, and the difference is countable.

The rest cover etymology, word concreteness, prosodic rhythm, psychological tone, syntactic structure, and readability, each resting on a published source the same way.

Tolerances

A fingerprint is a tolerance, not a score

A band is what a surface has earned the right to sit inside, computed from its own shipped strings; only a reading outside it counts as a miss. A profile applies within one context only: averaging a button, a procedure step, and a paragraph together describes your sampling, not the surface's voice. And dimensions that need more text than a four-word button report as unavailable rather than low, on purpose: a suppressed reading and a poor one look identical in a row of numbers and mean opposite things.

The spec

The rule from the pine card, as a build artifact

The engine's tool surface survives as a two-layer specification whose own headings enforce the sorting rule. Layer 1 put every checkable thing into one of twenty CLI tools returning JSON: linting for capitalization, punctuation, terminology, accessibility, and inclusive language; tone, readability, and conciseness scoring; error-message and notification linting with character limits; prosody down to stress clashes; the full fingerprint; the baseline comparison. Layer 2 hands the model what belongs to judgment: voice alignment, clarity, hierarchy, alternatives with rationale.

Three of the twenty tools point at the agent itself: verso_self_lint checks its own output, validate_rewrites lints proposed rewrites before delivery, and store_review writes every review to an audit trail. The self-check wasn't bolted on; it was named infrastructure.

The Content Index

Judgment stayed in the prompt. Everything checkable became a record.

A yes-or-no rule belongs in code; a matter of taste stays with the model. Once the deterministic half was pulled out of the guidance, what remained in the prompt was smaller than anyone expected.

What I did

How I built the Index

  • A record grammar before a database. I designed the entry shape every record shares: a versioned id, the assertion, and the routing metadata of scope, severity, owner, and source.
  • The judgment sort. I pulled every deterministic rule out of prose guidance and into checkable records, and left matters of taste in the prompt where they belong.
  • Real corpora, indexed. I consolidated scattered guidance per product area and grounded the profiles in shipped strings, including a Threads baseline built from 3,799 production strings alongside 36 company-wide standards.
  • Self-maintenance by diff. When a designer corrected a cited standard mid-conversation, Verso generated a diff of the proposed change and routed it to that space's content-design owner. Nothing was approved automatically, and the index grew past what I could scrape myself.

The Agent consulted the record and the Engine measured against it. What became a target stayed a human call.

The record, working · sources, enforcement, and coverage

The problem

The guidance existed. It just wasn't anywhere a tool could reach.

Real guidance was scattered across docs, chat threads, spreadsheets, and posts, on top of central brand guidance. Consolidating it per product area came first; sieving out what was actually deterministic came second.

In action

One prompt, twenty-three sources, and a correction on the record

Asked “In Horizon, what's the correct term: world, space, or environment?”, the agent searched the design-system index, noticed it had pulled from the wrong design system, said so, re-searched Horizon's own, and cross-referenced the terminology tool. The answer came back as a sourced table: Horizon Worlds capitalized for the app on first mention, worlds lowercase for an individual world, Spaces deprecated, personal space lowercase because it's a description, and Meta Horizon Home as the headset OS home environment.

23 sources consulted. Five steps. One prompt.

Enforcement

A record carries its checkability, and the checkability decides who enforces it

A terminology rule (use “reply,” not “comment,” in Threads conversations) is lintable, severity error: a linter's to enforce. A requirement (do not promise engagement, reach, growth, or virality) is a review blocker: no regex can rule on it. A fingerprint record (a notifications profile built from 130 live strings) is measurement, severity advice: owned by the system, because production strings, not a person, are its source.

Coverage

One profile per surface, not one profile for a product

The five flagships were fingerprinted first, from their own shipped strings: Instagram, WhatsApp, Meta Horizon, Quest, and Facebook. A shared brand voice still sounds different in a chat app than in a headset: same floor, a different band on top. The same pipeline then filled out the rest of Meta's products.

The handoff

The index went company-wide in about six months

From one product area to company-wide in roughly six months, entirely on word of mouth, before central content design formally took it on. They own it now. A rebuilt version of the same shape exists in the open: a public engine, working papers, and a deterministic prose scanner. None of it depends on Meta or contains anything of Meta's. It's the discipline, not the record.
