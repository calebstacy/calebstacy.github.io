I’m a designer and a word nerd, based in Richmond, Virginia. My specialty is agents who produce content and the rules that govern them. I taught college writing and poetry before I became a designer, but my ear for prosody came with me. I never stopped thinking about the system beneath words.

After finishing an MFA and teaching for a few years, I moved into content design at 8x8, Digbi Health, and Meta. At Meta, I worked on Horizon, led content experimentation, and built Verso.

Writing and education

I’ve studied poems my whole life. I earned a BA in English at West Virginia University in 2014 and an MFA in poetry at Virginia Commonwealth University in 2018. Prosody, the patterns of stress and rhythm in language, has been a particular interest.

While I was at VCU, I worked on Blackbird, the university’s online literary journal. In spring 2016, I was a literary intern and pagebuilder. By spring 2017, I was the lead pagebuilder and a production intern, leading the team that built the issue’s pages.

I taught composition and poetry at WVU and VCU, and writing and communication to Army officers at the Army Logistics University. From there, I moved into UX writing and content design.

Design career

8x8 and Digbi Health

At 8x8, I worked as a UX writer on phone administration and contact-center setup. Part of the work was helping business owners configure services without an IT department. From October 2021 to October 2022, I was lead UX writer at Digbi Health, where I led product content explaining microbiome science and clinical genetics to members.

Meta

Main articles: Portals and Meta Horizon NUX. See also: Work samples.

I worked at Meta from November 2022 to May 2026. On Horizon, I designed content for social coordination, trust and safety, onboarding, and travel across VR and mobile. I also led experimentation for Horizon’s content designers and served as an AI lead.

For Portals, I developed the content strategy for helping people understand a destination before entering it. For Horizon’s first-run experience, I developed principles for teaching controls when people needed them, rather than presenting a curriculum before they could join a world. I also worked on calls and parties, sharing, travel errors, and coordinated legal notices.

My experimentation work helped content designers develop hypotheses, plan tests, and share what they learned. I ran workshops and developed reusable templates and shared trackers for Horizon teams.

Verso

Main article: Verso

I initiated and built Verso at Meta during 2025 and 2026. It combined a conversational agent, a structured index of guidance and voice fingerprints, and an engine for language analysis. A designer could work through alternatives with the agent, investigate a content decision, and draw on research and product knowledge. The integrated system could also check its own proposed content before making a recommendation.

I designed and built the agent, taxonomy, Index, and Engine, revising them with feedback from content designers. As of November 2026, designers and cross-functional partners continue to use the system at Meta.

Poetry and language

My interest in content systems comes out of poetry. When I study a poem, I’m paying attention to particular features of the language and how they contribute to what I feel when I read it. Prosody gives me a way to name and examine some of those features. That interest came with me into design.

In my work on voice, I look at what language does: how it makes a request, directs attention, uses rhythm, or makes an idea concrete. In Verso, I built a taxonomy of linguistic dimensions and used it to compare language with relevant product references. What those measurements meant depended on the product, the situation, and the language used to establish the reference.

Research

Language instruments

My current work includes Word Math and Slop No More. Word Math is a research prototype for measuring features of reference writing, using them to define targets, and checking drafts against those targets. Slop No More checks configured patterns in English prose and reports findings that a writer can inspect and edit.

These days, you’re more likely to find me in VS Code than a Google Doc, building language instruments that turn an agent’s instruction files into little engines that check its work instead of little guides it can ignore.
