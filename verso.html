AI content governance · Meta · 2025–2026

Verso

Good guidance is written for people. Agents need a checkable target.

Meta’s content design org was moving from writing every string to governing what agents wrote.

  • Every week another content designer had built their own agent: a prompt file shaped like software, and a prompt file shaped like software fails like software. Quietly, with nothing built in to say so.
  • Review boards were the industry’s answer to agent output, at Meta as everywhere: people reading drafts and deciding whether they met the bar. It was the responsible move with the tools that existed. But a review board is a linter made of humans: slow, and no more consistent than the thing it’s checking.

I built Verso to give agents the target while keeping the kinds of authority separate.

What made Verso different

A content design agent that thinks like a coworker, not a linter.

Most AI writing helpers grade grammar. I built Verso to do content design: reason about user state, surface, product pattern, and evidence, then say what it would ship and why. Words are UI. Bad content is a bug.

Design insight

Voice was written in adjectives. I turned it into a target an agent could be held to. Guidance such as “concise” and “genuine” described what a reader feels, not what a writer or agent does.

How it worked

I built Verso to bring judgment to the work, not another score to decode.

  1. 01

    It’s a teammate, not a tool.

    I designed Verso to answer like a senior content designer pulled into a messy thread. It gave an opinion, the reason behind it, and a next move, not a score to decode.

  2. 02

    Opinions backed by evidence, not vibes.

    I grounded every recommendation in governed guidance, product patterns, or shipped strings. When the evidence ran out, Verso said so instead of bluffing.

  3. 03

    It runs real checks before it opens its mouth.

    I built the ten-dimension engine to test voice, directness, rhythm, density, and other language signals. Designers got the human translation, not raw JSON.

  4. 04

    It knows the difference between jamming and reviewing.

    I made its posture match the task. For brainstorming, it explored. For an audit, it structured the work, cited its evidence, and showed its reasoning.

  5. 05

    It gates the risky move, never the useful work.

    I let Verso draft, research, and analyze freely. I gated only consequential actions such as sending, publishing, or shipping, with the artifact already prepared for approval.

What was genuinely new

I made product judgment reusable without pretending it was a rule.

Fingerprint-calibrated voice

I calibrated each voice fingerprint to a product’s measured baseline, so voice became a target instead of a feeling.

Pattern-first instinct

I made it look for a proven shipped pattern before inventing a new one. Consistency beat cleverness.

Source-privacy awareness

I designed it to distinguish private notes from team guidance and not quietly carry private content into a wider audience.

Right-sized effort

I sized the work to the request: a bounded review for one string, the full machinery for an audit, and no research project for a gut check.

The bottom line

I treated content as a design surface and built Verso to run the checks, remember patterns, cite sources, and still exercise the judgment to say what it would ship.
